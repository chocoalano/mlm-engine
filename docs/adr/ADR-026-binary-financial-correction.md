# ADR-026: A binary reversal takes back its share of the stored commission, cumulatively floored, before or after posting

## Status

Accepted — Phase 3.4C. Closes Phase 3.4.

## Context

A reversal of a source that fed binary pairs undoes those pairs (ADR-025): a journal records how much of each historical pairing stopped counting, and names the commission the pairing earned. The commission still stands. A binary commission has no single source, so the source-entry clawback (ADR-021), which negates a commission whole, does not fit: a reversal may undo only part of the quantity a commission was paid for. The correction must be exact, never exceed what was paid, never rewrite history, and stay safe when posting, pairing runs and corrections happen in any order and concurrently.

## Decision

- **The stored amount is the financial truth.** A commission's share is apportioned from its stored `amount` (A) and its pairing result's stored `consumed_quantity` (Q), by the quantity the journal says was undone (I). No strategy runs again: no pair count, pair quantity, amount per pair, unit amount, rounding mode or genealogy. A fixed commission is corrected like a proportional one, and a proportional one from its rounded stored amount.
- **Cumulative floor.** Everything undone through a reversal is worth ⌊A × I ÷ Q⌋ financial millionths; a reversal's correction is that target less the one before it. The reversals that undid part of one pairing are ordered by their moment, then their id, so a correction comes out the same whichever reversal is processed first. Rounding each correction on its own would drift (three thirds of 100 would leave 0.000001 behind); the cumulative targets never add up to more than A and reach A exactly once all of Q is undone. A pair undone on one side releases the same quantity on the other, so I never exceeds Q; a journal that says otherwise is refused. Floor is the allocation's own rule, not the plan's rounding mode: it divides an amount already awarded, it does not award one. All exact, in decimal digits, with the pairing arithmetic's own multiplication and division; finance does not depend on binary.
- **`BinaryFinancialCorrectionAllocator`** is read-only: for a reversal's corrections and the commissions they name, it reads the pairing results and their whole correction history in one query each and returns, per commission, Q, A, I before and after, both targets and the correction.
- **One adjustment per commission and reversal.** `CommissionAdjustmentEngine::processBinaryReversal()` writes `clawback` adjustments of source `binary-volume-reversal` and the reversal's id, in the existing table and under its existing unique identity. Several journal rows of one reversal for one commission make one adjustment; their ids are listed, sorted, in its trace. The amount is the correction negated — **zero** when it is under a financial millionth: a zero adjustment is still written, so the reversal is known to be processed and a replay returns it. A pairing whose award rounded to nothing has no commission and needs no adjustment. Two outcomes are added: `recorded` (no money moved) and `adjusted` (money moved back in a transaction of its own).
- **Net amount.** A commission's net amount is its calculated amount plus all its adjustments (`CommissionNetAmount`); it must lie between zero and the calculated amount, or it is refused as corrupt.
- **Before posting — CALCULATED, PENDING, APPROVED.** A correction moves no money. If the net amount is still positive, the commission keeps its status: `recorded`. If nothing is left, it is cancelled through `CommissionLifecycle`: `cancelled`.
- **Posting pays the net amount, and remembers it.** `CommissionPoster::post()` moves the net amount, not the calculated one, and records it in `posted_amount_millionths` (migration 000030); the calculated amount never changes. Commissions posted before this phase were posted whole, and the migration records that for every POSTED or REVERSED commission. A posted commission is verified against its posted amount, and `reverse()` reverses exactly what was posted. An approved commission whose net is zero is never posted for nothing: it is refused.
- **An unresolved correction blocks posting.** While a journal row names a commission and no adjustment exists for its reversal, `post()` refuses with `UnresolvedBinaryCorrection` — so a stale binary commission is never paid by accident. Processing the reversal and posting again pays what is left.
- **After posting — POSTED.** A zero correction moves nothing: `recorded`. A correction that leaves nothing, when no part of the commission has been moved back before, reverses its posting through `CommissionPoster::reverse()` at the reversal's moment: `reversed`. Any other correction — part of it, or the rest after an earlier partial one — moves back through `CommissionAdjustmentPoster`: one balanced ledger transaction, the wallet debited and the run's source account credited by exactly the correction, occurring at the reversal's moment, of type and source type `commission-adjustment`, identified by the commission and the reversal — never the clock — so a replay cannot move money twice: `adjusted`. The commission stays POSTED; its original posting stays history. Reversing the original after an earlier partial correction would over-correct, so it is never done — and `CommissionPoster::reverse()` refuses a commission already partly corrected through the ledger. The wallet may go negative: the ledger has no balance floor, and payout and debt policy are later designs. Before each movement, what the wallet still holds of the commission — its posted amount less what was moved back — is checked to equal its net amount.
- **CANCELLED, REVERSED.** Nothing changes: `already_cancelled`, or `already_reversed` once the existing reversal is verified.
- **Explicit orchestration.** Nothing runs automatically. The application reverses volume, runs the pairing calculation that takes the reversal in (which writes the journal, ADR-025), then calls `processBinaryReversal()`. The calculation never touches the ledger; the financial step is retryable on its own; the application controls the order; and the posting guard stops a commission being paid in between. Calling before the run has committed finds nothing and fakes nothing; calling again later finds the journal. Nothing marks a reversal done for good.
- **Atomic, replayable, concurrent.** One call applies every correction it finds, or none. It locks the reversal, then the original, reads the reversal's journal under a shared lock, then locks the commissions in id order — and only then reads anything else. On MySQL the first plain read of a transaction fixes what later plain reads see; reading the journal without a lock let a second reversal's call miss what a first had just recorded, leaving a commission with nothing left uncancelled, as a real two-session test showed. Two calls for one reversal serialize on its row: one adjustment and at most one movement. A post racing a correction serializes on the commission's row: if the correction wins, the post pays what is left or finds the commission cancelled; if the post wins, it finds the correction unresolved and is refused. Retried, both orders end with the same money moved, once.
- **History is never rewritten.** Runs, pairing results, allocations, the correction journal, carry lots and cursors are untouched; so are the commission's calculated facts — amount, member, key, earned-at, trace. `CalculationEngine` is unchanged.

## Consequences

- Phase 3.4 is complete: binary placement, temporal genealogy, leg metrics, pairing, carry, fixed and proportional awards, consumed-source correction and its financial correction are integrated, replayable, audited and concurrency-safe.
- Applications must call `processBinaryReversal()` after the pairing run that takes a reversal in; until they do, the commissions it affects cannot be posted.
- A commission's calculated amount is no longer what posting necessarily paid: read `posted_amount` for that, and `CommissionNetAmount` for what it is still worth.
- Negative wallet balances after a correction, payouts and debt recovery, manual and positive adjustments, and late binary events remain later designs.
