# ADR-022: Binary is an explicit left/right overlay on generic placement, with its own paths and leg metrics

## Status

Accepted — Phase 3.2.

## Context

Binary plans pay on two legs: everything below a member's left child, and everything below its right child. The generic placement tree (ADR-008) records where a member sits but deliberately knows no positions — a parent may have any number of children — so it cannot say which child is left, and its paths cannot say which leg a descendant is in. Binary needs sides, a limit of one child per side, and leg reads that are as historically exact as the network metrics (ADR-013), without making placement itself binary and without guessing positions for placements that already exist.

## Decision

- **Generic placement stays positionless.** `PlacementGenealogy` and `mlm_placement_edges` are unchanged: no side, slot or capacity, and a parent still takes any number of children. Matrix, hybrid and other structures can later add their own overlays the same way.
- **Binary is an overlay.** A binary position assigns one existing generic placement edge to its parent's `left` or `right`, in `mlm_binary_placement_positions`: the edge (unique), its parent (always the edge's parent, kept for the slot key and the side lookup), the side, and `assigned_at`. The placed member is the edge's, not stored twice. A parent has at most one child on each side (`UNIQUE(parent_id, side)`); its other generic children are simply not in the binary tree.
- **Exact sides.** `BinarySide` is exactly `left` or `right`: no case changes, trimming, abbreviations, numbers or booleans, and never derived from the order children were placed in. The column compares exactly on MySQL.
- **Its own paths.** Binary ancestry is kept in `mlm_genealogy_paths` under `tree_type` `binary`, through the same `ClosureTree` mechanics — not derived from the placement paths, because a generic-only edge below a binary member must not put its subtree into a leg. Sponsor and placement paths are untouched by binary writes, and each genealogy reads only its own tree.
- **Place or adopt.** `BinaryPlacementManager::place()` places a member through `PlacementGenealogy::place()` — program, single-parent and cycle rules stay there alone — and assigns the new edge a side, in one transaction: a side that cannot be taken undoes the placement. `adopt()` assigns a side to an edge that already exists. Adopting again on the same side returns the position; on the other side it is refused. `place()` is not a replay: a member already placed is refused as placement refuses it.
- **Adoption is not retroactive.** A placement may be months old when it enters the binary tree; the binary relationship begins at its adoption, so earlier activity never becomes binary activity. `assigned_at` is the adoption moment, never before the edge's `placed_at`, and floored as every genealogy write is by the moments already in the two binary parts it joins (ADR-012). For `place()` it is the placement's own moment unless that floor is later. An adopted member brings its whole binary subtree, whose paths below it keep their own moments.
- **Only growth, only explicit.** A side is assigned once: there is no move, no removal, no expiry, and no automatic placement — no spillover, next free slot or weaker-side rule. The caller names member, parent and side.
- **Writes serialize per program.** A binary write locks the edge, its members and then the program, like every genealogy write, and reads positions with locking reads: two writes onto one side leave one child and a focused `InvalidBinaryPlacement`, never a raw constraint error, and a losing `place()` leaves no generic edge behind. Opposite sides both succeed.
- **One binary tree per program.** The overlay belongs to the program, not to a plan version or component: every binary plan of a program reads the same structure. Several independent binary trees in one program would need a structure identifier on every position and path; that is deferred until a plan needs it.
- **Reads trust the database, and refuse corruption.** `BinaryGenealogy` reads positions, parents, the child on a side, ancestors and descendants — now or as they stood at a moment — always from the stored member. A position whose parent differs from its edge's, whose side is spelled otherwise, or whose members are in another program is refused with `CorruptBinaryPlacement`, never read into a network or repaired.
- **Leg metrics.** `binary.left.volume` and `binary.right.volume` are plan-configurable metrics with exactly `type` (the volume type, checked as every volume metric checks it) and an optional `max_depth` (an integer of at least 1, counted from the anchor: 1 is the child on that side alone). A leg is the child on that side and the child's binary subtree; the anchor's own volume, the other leg and generic-only descendants never count, and an empty side is 0.
- **Historical attribution.** An entry falls in the period of its own `effective_at`, `[from, until)`. It counts in a leg only if, at its attribution moment — its own `effective_at`, or for a reversal the reversed entry's — the anchor's side had been assigned and the binary path from the child to the entry's member had taken effect. Both are required: the child's own paths may be older than its place under the anchor. So a side assigned later, or a subtree adopted later, never captures earlier activity; a reversal lands in the leg its activity landed in, in its own period; and an activity and its reversal in one period net out. One side lookup and one aggregate query over the binary paths and the volume history compute a leg: nothing is loaded member by member or stored. The existing indexes serve it — the paths' primary key and depth index, the entries' member/type/moment and program/type/source/moment indexes — so no migration adds one. Measured over a 32,767-member binary tree and 306,000 entries, PostgreSQL 18 plans it well unaided (a 16,383-member leg over a month in 100 ms, over the year in 57 ms, a 127-member leg in 0.9 ms). MySQL 9.6, like the network metrics (ADR-013), reads a large leg by walking the program's every entry through the `(program_id, idempotency_key)` key: 822 ms for the month and 1,391 ms for the year. Told to ignore that one package-owned key it takes 107 and 300 ms, and small legs are unchanged (1.1 ms), so the leg query carries the same hint.
- **No pairing yet.** Pair units, carry-forward, carry expiry, pair limits, flush rules and how they cross plan versions need persisted calculation state designed on purpose. There is no binary commission strategy and no carry or pairing table.

## Consequences

- Binary plans get sides, legs and exact historical leg volume on the existing structure, and qualification and rank rules can use the legs with no change to their engines.
- Existing generic placements stay generic until an application decides their sides: the migration only creates empty storage and backfills nothing.
- Binary writes share the program's genealogy lock, so they run one at a time within a program.
- Rolling back migration 000022 removes the positions and the binary paths with them.
- Pairing and carry, automatic placement and multiple binary trees per program remain later designs.
