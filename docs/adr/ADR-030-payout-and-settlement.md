# ADR-030: Payouts reserve wallet funds through the ledger at approval and record an external settlement

## Status

Accepted — Phase 3.8.

## Context

Posted commissions credit member wallets through the ledger (ADR-016, ADR-018), but nothing lets a member's funds leave the system: no withdrawal request, no review before money is committed, no protection against paying the same funds twice, no record of the provider's transfer, and no way back when that transfer fails. Operators also pay out in groups. Money must keep moving only through the ledger, and the wallet must stay the one source of truth: commission, period, calculation and genealogy history are not payout inputs.

## Decision

- **The wallet is the payout's source of truth.** A payout is funded by the member's wallet balance — the exact sum of its ledger postings (`LedgerBalanceReader`) — whatever credited it. A payout never reads commissions, commission periods, calculation runs or the genealogy, and is never linked to a commission. `CommissionStatus` gains no paid status: posted still means the ledger credited the wallet, and paying the wallet out is a separate fact.
- **A payout request.** `mlm_payout_requests` (000038): program, member, wallet, settlement account, currency, amount in millionths, an opaque destination (`destination_type`, an identifier, and `destination_reference`, printable text up to 191 characters), idempotency key, status, the moment of each step, settlement reference, failure reason, and the reservation and refund ledger transactions. `PayoutManager::request()` checks a positive amount within the ledger's range, the member's own wallet in the program, exactly one wallet account in the currency, and a settlement account that is a **system account** of the same program and currency. The destination is provider-agnostic and never holds credentials: the host resolves it. The same key with the same facts returns the request; other facts are refused (`ConflictingPayoutRequest`). Creating one takes the program's lock. Requesting moves no money. Models are read-only through Eloquent.
- **Lifecycle.** `requested → approved → processing → settled`, `requested → cancelled`, and `approved` or `processing → failed`. Forward only, each step once, each at an explicit moment; a step already taken with the same facts is returned, with other facts it is refused. A failed step leaves the request as it was.
- **Reservation at approval.** `approve()` locks the request, then the wallet's row, reads the balance only then and refuses an amount above it (`InsufficientPayoutBalance`). It posts one balanced ledger transaction — type `payout-reservation`, source `payout-request`, key `payout.reserve.<request>` — debiting the wallet account and crediting the settlement account by the amount, and moves the request to approved with that transaction. So the reservation is itself the movement: no available, reserved or pending balance column and no reservation table exist, and approved funds cannot be spent twice because the balance already excludes them. A payout of part of the balance leaves the rest in the wallet for later requests.
- **Processing and settlement.** `startProcessing()` marks the external transfer as begun. `settle()` records the provider's `settlement_reference` — unique within the program, so one external transfer settles one request — and the settlement moment. Settlement writes **no second ledger movement**: the money left the wallet at approval, and the settlement account holds it until the host's own books clear it.
- **Failure refunds.** `fail()`, from approved or processing, reverses the reservation through `LedgerRecorder::reverse()` — key `payout.refund.<request>`, at the failure moment — restoring the wallet exactly, and stores the refund transaction and the reason. The reservation itself is never rewritten.
- **Cancellation** is for requests not yet approved: nothing was reserved, so nothing moves; the reason is kept.
- **Verification.** Every step reads the request's ledger transactions back and checks them against its status (`PayoutLedger`): no reservation while requested or cancelled; a reservation of exactly `{wallet: −amount, settlement: +amount}` under its identity from approved on; a refund reversing exactly that reservation only when failed; a settlement reference only when settled. Anything else is `CorruptPayoutRequest`, and the step writes nothing.
- **Batches group, never move money.** `mlm_payout_batches` (000039) and `mlm_payout_batch_items` (000040): one program and currency, an idempotency key, `open → sealed → processing → completed`, or `open → cancelled`. `PayoutBatchManager` adds approved requests of the batch's program and currency to an open batch at the next position; a request belongs to one batch at most. Sealing needs at least one request. Starting a sealed batch moves every request — all still approved — to processing at once, all or none; a batched request never starts on its own while its batch is open or sealed. Each request then settles or fails on its own, and the batch completes once none is still processing — a partly failed batch completes too. `PayoutBatchTotals::of($batch)` gives counts and requested, reserved, settled and failed totals, exactly, in one aggregate query.
- **Lock order.** Program for creation. A request step locks the request, then — for approval — the wallet, then writes the ledger; it never locks a batch, only reads its status. Batch operations lock the batch, then its requests in id order. So approve, batch start, settle and fail never wait on each other in opposite orders; settle and fail of one request serialize on its row, and exactly one of them wins. Every concurrency-sensitive read comes after the lock that guards it, so MySQL's repeatable-read snapshot is never stale when funds are checked.

## Consequences

- Funds leave a wallet only through an approved payout's explicit ledger reservation, never twice, and come back only by reversing it.
- A commission clawed back after its funds were paid out does not rewrite the payout: the clawback debits the wallet, which may go negative. Recovering that balance is a later concern.
- Deferred: payment provider SDKs, HTTP calls, webhooks and automatic retry; bank account management, KYC and AML; taxes, fees, minimum and maximum payout policies; currency conversion and cross-currency payouts; scheduled automatic payouts; bank exports, payment files and reconciliation import; chargebacks; a commission paid status and per-commission payout allocation; and the Panda Panel payout screens.
