# ADR-021: A later source reversal claws back the commissions actually stored, through their lifecycle and the ledger, and can be replayed

## Status

Accepted — Phase 3.1. Amended in Phase 3.4C: `CommissionAdjustment` also represents partial binary financial corrections — source `binary-volume-reversal`, a share of the stored amount that may be zero, outcomes `recorded` and `adjusted` (ADR-026). Source-entry clawback is unchanged.

## Context

The built-in strategies pay per original volume entry (ADR-019, ADR-020), and a reversal after a run's cutoff leaves that run's commissions standing — deliberately, so a historical run stays reproducible. But the money should come back: a commission earned from a sale that was later refunded must be corrected. The correction must be exactly what was awarded, however the plan, the genealogy or the rounding look today; it must never rewrite what a calculation stored; and it must stay correct when calculations and corrections happen in any order.

## Decision

- **Relational provenance.** A commission may record the business record it was earned from, as `source_type` and `source_id` — both or neither — through an optional `CommissionSourceReference` on its candidate. The calculation engine stores it generically, knowing no strategy. The four built-in source-entry strategies record `volume-entry` and the original entry's id; their traces are unchanged. Traces are audit output: finding commissions by searching JSON would be slow, engine-specific and only as trustworthy as every strategy's trace shape, so the lookup is an index over `(source_type, source_id, program_id)`. Program is last so a lookup can also find provenance that crosses programs — and refuse it — while program-scoped reads are served as well. Measured over 300,000 commissions in three programs, the clawback's locking lookup by source is an index lookup on MySQL 9.6 and PostgreSQL 18 (0.26 and 0.14 ms). Led by program instead, the index serves it on neither without help: MySQL scans the primary key (177 ms) and, as a locking read, would lock every row scanned; PostgreSQL falls back to a skip scan, one search per program.
- **An opt-in contract.** A strategy that records `volume-entry` for a commission declares it earned wholly from that one original entry, and opts it into full clawback when the entry is reversed. Commissions without provenance are never touched.
- **Existing commissions are backfilled, strictly.** Migration 000020 gives commissions of runs of exactly `direct-sponsor.fixed`, `direct-sponsor.proportional`, `unilevel.fixed` and `unilevel.proportional` the entry their trace names — in chunks, through the query builder, with no engine-specific JSON SQL. The entry must exist, belong to the commission's program and be an original, or the migration stops, writes no provenance, and can be rerun once the row is corrected: MySQL keeps a failed run's columns, so the rerun skips them and fills only rows still empty. Other strategies' commissions are never guessed from their traces.
- **Adjustments are immutable records.** A `CommissionAdjustment` records one correction of one commission: its type — `clawback`, the only one now — its source — `volume-entry-reversal` and the reversal's id — a signed amount, the business moment, the outcome, the ledger reversal when money moved back, and a canonical trace. It is written once, when the correction completes, by `CommissionAdjustmentEngine`, and read-only through Eloquent. There is no adjustment lifecycle: a stored adjustment is a completed correction.
- **The stored amount, negated — nothing recalculated.** A clawback's amount is the commission's stored amount, negated, exactly: no strategy, genealogy, quantity-to-money conversion, rounding or plan version is consulted, and the recipient is the commission's member. A proportional commission is clawed back by its rounded stored amount. The commission's calculated facts — run, program, member, key, currency, amount, earned-at, trace, provenance — never change, and neither does its run. No negative commission, and no second run, is ever created.
- **The outcome follows the commission's status.** CALCULATED, PENDING and APPROVED commissions moved no money, so they are cancelled through `CommissionLifecycle`: outcome `cancelled`, no ledger transaction — debiting a wallet that was never credited would be wrong. A POSTED commission is reversed through `CommissionPoster::reverse()` at the reversal's `effective_at`: outcome `reversed`, recording the ledger reversal. The adjustment engine never touches the ledger itself. A CANCELLED commission is left alone: `already_cancelled`. A REVERSED one is not reversed again: its existing ledger reversal is checked through `CommissionPoster::verifiedReversal()` and recorded, `already_reversed`, even if it happened at another moment. Every adjustment records the negated amount — the correction requested — and its outcome and ledger reference say whether money moved now.
- **One correction per commission and reversal, safely replayable.** An adjustment is identified by its commission, type, and source; the unique key is the backstop. `processVolumeReversal()` is explicit and idempotent: a call returns adjustments already made as they were made, and corrects commissions found since. Nothing marks a reversal done for good, because a historical run whose cutoff preceded the reversal may create commissions for the entry after an earlier call found none; calling again finds and corrects them. Calculation and correction do not lock each other: rerunning the processor is the design.
- **One transaction per call, in a fixed lock order.** The stored reversal, then its original, then the linked commissions in id order are locked; every correction is applied and every adjustment inserted, or none is — the lifecycle, poster and ledger nest inside as savepoints. The reversal is checked against its original as the volume history defines it — same program, member and type, exactly the negated quantity, an original — and a commission linked across programs is refused, never skipped. Two processors of one reversal serialize on the reversal's row. A post racing a clawback serializes on the commission's row: if the clawback wins, the commission is cancelled and the post refused; if the post wins, the clawback reverses it. It is never left posted and uncorrected, and money never moves twice.
- **Volume history triggers nothing.** `VolumeRecorder::reverse()` is unchanged. The application calls the processor after reversing volume — so a failure is visible and retryable, domains stay separate, a replay after a historical recalculation is natural, and recording volume never has a hidden financial side effect. There is no event listener, queue or configuration switch.

## Consequences

- A refunded sale's commissions come back exactly, whenever they were calculated and whatever their state, with an adjustment explaining each — and nothing already recorded is rewritten.
- Applications must call the processor after a reversal, and again after any historical recalculation of a range the reversal affects; calling it more often is harmless.
- Partial reversals, manual or positive adjustments, and automatic orchestration are later designs.
